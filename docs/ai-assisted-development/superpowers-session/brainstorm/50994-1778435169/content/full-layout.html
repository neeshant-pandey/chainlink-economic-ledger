Bitcoin Foundations · Lesson 4 of 9

What moves through Bitcoin?

Imagine you slide a $20 bill across a table. The bill itself doesn't change — only who holds it. Bitcoin works the same way, but the “bill” is a chain of digital signatures, and the table is a public ledger anyone can audit.

The unit of movement is called a transaction. It is the only thing Bitcoin actually stores. Blocks just bundle them.

A transaction in Bitcoin is not a debit on a balance sheet — it is the destruction of one set of IOUs and the creation of another.
A single Bitcoin transaction with one input, two outputs, and an implicit fee.

Bitcoin's design refuses the idea of an "account balance" altogether. Your wallet is just software that scans the ledger and shows you which IOUs are addressed to a key you control. Spending one is destructive: the IOU is consumed, and a new IOU is written for the recipient.
